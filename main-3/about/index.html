I find the failure modes before they become legal exposure, reputational damage, or irreversible compliance gaps. Every document has a structural kill. The question is whether you find it — or your counterparty does.

Who I Am

I'm Sheldon K. Salmon. I work alone, with AI as my thinking partner. I built AionSystem to do one thing: find what kills a document before it costs you.

Most document failure is not caused by careless authors. It is caused by authors reading their own work as supporters, not adversaries. The clause that seems clear to the drafter is exploitable by the party on the other side of the table. The obligation that seems enforceable in isolation becomes legally void when you trace its dependency chain to an undefined term in an annex.

I read every document as the opposing party. Not to find rhetorical weaknesses — to find the structural failure modes: undefined thresholds, missing enforcement mechanisms, circular dependencies, obligations stated without triggering conditions.

The findings come with ready-to-insert resolution language. Not a problem list. A corrected document.

What AionSystem Is

AionSystem is the practice name for my adversarial document review work. It is not an agency. It is not a team. It is one analyst and a set of formal instruments built to find structural failure nodes in contracts, policies, frameworks, specifications, and filings.

The name comes from Aion — time, duration, the age of a thing. The point is simple: a document that survives contact with reality is a document built to last. A document that doesn't is a liability waiting for the wrong moment to detonate.

The Advocatus Diaboli

I carry the title Advocatus Diaboli — the Devil's Advocate — as a method, not an office. The Catholic Church employed the Promotor Fidei for centuries as the official whose sole duty was to argue against the evidence before it became doctrine. In 1983, the Church kept the office but removed the adversarial function.

No secular equivalent replaced it. So I built one. The Advocatus Diaboli Protocol is a seven-check instrument reconstructed from the historical method, tested against my own work and against the EU AI Act itself.

I don't claim the ecclesiastical office. I claim the function. The full reconstruction is published with a DOI.

Operating Principles

  • Adversarial Reading First. Every document is read as the opposing party before it is read as anything else. The author's intent is secondary to the document's actual operative effect.

  • Structural Findings Only. Surface-level drafting errors, style inconsistencies, and rhetorical weaknesses are not the work. Structural failure nodes — the places where the document's own logic breaks — are the work.

  • Resolution Text Always. A finding without resolution language is half a deliverable. Every failure node ships with ready-to-insert language that closes the gap while preserving the drafter's intent.

  • Severity Must Be Named. Not all findings are equal. Critical gaps that make obligations legally unenforceable are not in the same register as minor drafting inconsistencies. The register difference must be explicit, not left to the client to infer.

  • Independence Is the Asset. An internal review team reads the document it helped write. An independent analyst reads it without the context that made the gaps invisible to the authors. Independence is not a credential — it is the mechanism of the method.

On the Instrument Stack

The methodology is not one instrument — it is a stack. The adversarial read identifies candidate failure nodes. The epistemic tagging protocol classifies each finding by confidence basis. The severity scoring system distinguishes Critical from Major from Minor by the downstream consequence of each gap activating. The resolution protocol closes each gap with language that survives the same adversarial read that found it. The stack is formal, versioned, and applied consistently across every engagement.
