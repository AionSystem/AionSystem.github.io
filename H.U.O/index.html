Heterogeneity Under Observability

An Acquisition-Cost Framework for Assessing Diversity as a Cyber-Physical Defense

Sheldon K. Salmon · AionSystem · Response to DARPA-SN-27-17

Full paper (PDF) · Submission version, 8 pages (PDF) This page is a supplement to the paper. The paper states the argument; the interactive model and scenes below are a worked example of the §4.1 formula, the §7.2–§7.3 metric distinction, and the §4.6 illustrations.

Summary

The FLASH RFI asks whether heterogeneity reduces adversary knowledge transfer. This response shows that the answer depends on the adversary's acquisition model. Heterogeneity multiplies the adversary's cost by up to a factor of N when the adversary must acquire each variant separately (class-attacking A₁, capture-then-exploit A₂-hybrid), and does nothing when the adversary probes the deployed instance in real time (A₂-pure). The framework supplies a metric — Hd_variant — that predicts the multiplier, an evaluation protocol that tests the metric against a calibrated exploit-development agent, and a set of declared assumptions under which the argument holds.

Interactive model of the §4.1 formula and its two metrics (§7.2–§7.3)

The formula in §4.1 gives the adversary's cost under A₂-hybrid as a function of two quantities computed from the same fleet: Hd_node (the fraction of critical failure nodes that are variant-specific) and Hd_variant (the fraction of variants whose cheapest path to compromise is variant-specific). The formula uses Hd_variant. When a fleet has a cheap shared node, the two metrics diverge (§7.3), and using Hd_node in the formula overstates the adversary's burden by up to a factor of N.

Model scope. This model binarizes Hd_variant to {0, 1} based on whether the shared node is the cheapest path to compromise; the paper's formula treats Hd_variant as continuous over [0, 1]. The model uses unit values for c_capture and c_exploit so that cost equals cycle count; the paper's formula allows arbitrary magnitudes. The model assumes a single transfer cluster; the paper's Assumption 7 addresses multi-cluster cases. Where the model's display and the paper disagree, the paper governs.

Cost = [1 + H_d_variant · (N − 1)] × (c_capture + c_exploit) The model sets (c_capture + c_exploit) = 1 so cost equals cycle count.

The scenes of §4.6

Five scenes from the paper (§4.6), shown as static diagrams. Scene 1 is the baseline. Scene 2 is the ceiling. Scene 3 is the intermediate case. Scene 4 is the case where apparent heterogeneity collapses to Hd_variant = 0. Scene 5 is the case where capture is sublinear in N.

Scene 1 — homogeneous fleet, Hd_variant = 0

All doors share one lock. The adversary picks it once, studies it, and opens every door. Cost: 1 × (c_capture + c_exploit).

Scene 2 — fully heterogeneous fleet, Hd_variant = 1

Every door has a distinct lock. The adversary's technique for door 1 does not open door 2. The building is compromised for the cost of N distinct lock picks: N × (c_capture + c_exploit). At Hd_variant = 1, the formula's multiplier reaches its maximum.

Scene 3 — mixed fleet, Hd_variant = 0.3, N = 10

Ten variant types, ten instances each. Three types have unique locks (30 instances); seven types share one lock (70 instances). The adversary picks four locks total — three unique, one shared — and compromises all ten variants (100 instances). Cycle count: 1 + 0.3·(10 − 1) = 3.7, rounded up to 4.

Scene 4 — apparent heterogeneity, Hd_node ≈ 1 but Hd_variant = 0

The hulls, sensor suites, and vendor names differ. The firmware does not. The adversary's exploit transfers, and Hd_node reports a differentiated fleet while the formula correctly reports a homogeneous one.

Scene 5 — shared hallway, capture is sublinear in N

One door leads to a corridor that reaches every room. The adversary captures one door (c_capture) and studies all four variants reachable through the corridor without a separate physical capture. The effective per-variant capture cost is c_capture / k_cap, where k_cap is the average number of variants reachable per capture. This is still A₂-hybrid: capture happens, then study; the topology reduces the cost, not the class.

Scope and declared assumptions

The paper declares its assumptions and its limitations. The summary below is a one-paragraph version; the full list is in §12 of the paper.

Assumptions. The argument holds when four conditions are met: (1) the family has genuine degrees of freedom that provably preserve required behavior; (2) the adversary's acquisition model is A₁ or A₂-hybrid, not A₂-pure; (3) capture and exploit costs are independent across variants, with no transfer missed by the §7.2 independence test; and (4) the Hd_variant used in the formula is the post-test, post-reduction value, not the raw node ratio.

Limitations. The metric is analyst-dependent at three judgment points (L9). The evaluation protocol is proposed, not run. The framework has not been tested against a real fleet. The anchor domain is UUV/USV, named from public regulatory documents (COLREGs, 33 CFR Part 83); the response does not claim program participation, fleet data, or operational experience in the domain.

What would falsify the argument. Flat time-to-compromise under A₂-hybrid; Hd_variant uncorrelated with documented failure history; the A₁ structural multiplier not shared with A₂-hybrid. These are stated in §12 and Annex B of the paper.
