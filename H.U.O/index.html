Summary

The FLASH RFI asks whether heterogeneity reduces adversary knowledge transfer. The answer depends on the adversary’s acquisition model. Heterogeneity multiplies the adversary’s cost by up to a factor of N when the adversary must acquire each variant separately (class-attacking A₁, capture-then-exploit A₂-hybrid). For static or slow-dynamic heterogeneity it does not change the cost when the adversary probes the deployed instance in real time (A₂-pure). The paper proposes a metric, Hd_variant, that predicts the multiplier; a method for determining which adversary class applies to a program (§2.1); an evaluation protocol that tests the prediction (§9); and a set of declared assumptions under which the argument holds.

Interactive model of the §4.1 formula and its two metrics (§7.2–§7.3)

The formula in §4.1 gives the adversary’s cost under A₂-hybrid as a function of two quantities computed from the same fleet: Hd_node (the fraction of critical failure nodes that are variant-specific) and Hd_variant (the fraction of variants whose cheapest path to compromise is variant-specific). The formula uses Hd_variant. When a fleet has a cheap shared node, the two diverge (§7.3), and using Hd_node in the formula overstates the adversary’s burden, by up to a factor of N. In the opposite structure it understates it (§7.2).

Model scope. This model sets Hd_variant to 0 or 1 according to whether the shared node is the cheapest path to compromise; the paper’s formula treats Hd_variant as continuous over [0, 1]. The model uses unit values for c_capture and c_exploit so that cost equals cycle count; the paper’s formula allows arbitrary magnitudes. The model assumes a single transfer cluster (Assumption 7) and one capture per variant (k_cap = 1, Assumption 6). Where the model and the paper differ, the paper governs.

Cycles = 1 + H_d_variant · (N − 1) Cost = ⌈Cycles⌉ × (c_capture + c_exploit) (k_cap = 1) The model sets (c_capture + c_exploit) = 1, so cost equals cycle count.

The scenes of §4.6

Five scenes from the paper (§4.6), shown as static diagrams. Doors are drawn as four for legibility; the paper’s scenes use 100 doors (Scenes 1, 2, 4) and ten variant types (Scene 3). Scene 1 is the baseline. Scene 2 is the ceiling. Scene 3 is the intermediate case. Scene 4 is the case where apparent heterogeneity collapses to Hd_variant = 0. Scene 5 is the case where captures grow more slowly than the cycle count.

Scene 1 — homogeneous fleet, Hd_variant = 0

All doors share one lock. The adversary picks it once, studies it, and opens every door. Cost: 1 × (c_capture + c_exploit).

Scene 2 — fully heterogeneous fleet, Hd_variant = 1

Every door has a distinct lock. The adversary’s technique for door 1 does not open door 2. The building is compromised for the cost of N distinct lock picks: N × (c_capture + c_exploit). At Hd_variant = 1, the formula’s multiplier reaches its maximum.

Scene 3 — mixed fleet, Hd_variant = 0.3, N = 10

Ten variant types, ten instances each. Three types have unique locks (30 instances); seven types share one lock (70 instances). The adversary picks four locks in total, three unique and one shared, and compromises all ten variants (100 instances). The formula gives 1 + 0.3·(10 − 1) = 3.7 cycles; the exact count is 0.3·10 + 1 = 4, the ceiling of 3.7 (§4.5).

Scene 4 — apparent heterogeneity, Hd_variant = 0

The hulls, sensor suites, and vendor names differ. The firmware does not. The adversary’s exploit transfers. A count that treats appearance differences as differentiation reports a diverse fleet; the §7.2 test finds the transfer, and the formula, using the post-test Hd_variant, reports a homogeneous one.

Scene 5 — shared hallway, captures grow more slowly than cycles

One door leads to a corridor that reaches every room. The adversary captures one door and studies all four variants reachable through the corridor without a separate physical capture. The number of captures is max(1, ⌈cycles / k_cap⌉), where k_cap is the average number of variants reachable per capture; c_exploit is still paid per cycle. This is still A₂-hybrid: capture happens, then study. Topology changes the capture term, not the class.

Scope and declared assumptions

The paper declares its assumptions and limitations in §12. This is a short version.

Assumptions. The argument holds when these conditions are met: (1) the family has genuine degrees of freedom that provably preserve required behavior; (2) the adversary’s acquisition class is A₁ or A₂-hybrid, determined per program by the method in §2.1; (3) the §7.2 test has identified every transfer between variants; (4) the Hd_variant used in the formula is the post-test, post-reduction value, not the raw node ratio; (5) the capture term is adjusted for deployment topology; and (6) the non-independent variants form a single transfer cluster.

Limitations. The correspondence between Hd_variant and adversary cost is a hypothesis (L1). The evaluation protocol is proposed, not run (L2). The metric is analyst-dependent at three judgment points (L9). The defender’s burden includes an Hd_variant computation that may grow as N² (L8). Exploitation through a shared specification or through specified in-envelope behavior is not priced by the formula (L10). The framework has not been tested on a real fleet. The anchor domain is UUV/USV, named from public regulatory documents (COLREGs, 33 CFR Part 83).

What would falsify the argument. Flat time-to-compromise under A₂-hybrid; Hd_variant uncorrelated with documented failure history; the A₁ structural multiplier not shared with A₂-hybrid. These are stated in §12 and Annex B of the paper.
